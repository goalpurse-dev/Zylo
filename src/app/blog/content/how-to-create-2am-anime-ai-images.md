## What makes an image read as "anime style"

Four visual choices separate anime-style output from generic cinematic AI art:

- **Line work.** Anime relies on clean, deliberate outlines rather than fully painterly rendering — even soft, painterly anime styles keep some edge definition.
- **Flat-to-soft shading.** Cel-shading (hard light/shadow blocks) or soft gradient shading, rather than photorealistic light physics.
- **Expressive skies and color.** Anime backgrounds often push sky and lighting color further than realism would — deep violets, saturated sunset oranges, glowing city neon.
- **Deliberate scale.** Anime compositions frequently emphasize how small a character is against a building, sky, or skyline — a signature way anime communicates mood.

Naming the sub-style directly (see below) gets you a far more specific result than "anime" alone.

## The 2AM anime prompt formula

**Anime sub-style + location + character presence + lighting + mood**

A short reference is enough — Zyvo's planner fills in the rest — but naming the sub-style is the single highest-leverage word in the prompt.

### Five anime sub-styles worth naming directly

| Sub-style | Visual signature | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Shounen action | Bold linework, dramatic sky, high contrast | City rooftops, training grounds, quiet-before-the-fight |
| Slice-of-life | Soft pastel light, gentle color, small detail | School hallways, convenience stores, bedrooms |
| Studio Ghibli-inspired | Painterly backgrounds, warm naturalism | Countryside, quiet towns, nature at night |
| Cyberpunk anime | Neon, rain-reflection, dense signage | Futuristic cities, back alleys, train platforms |
| Mecha anime | Industrial scale, hangar lighting, metal texture | Hangars, control rooms, launch bays |
