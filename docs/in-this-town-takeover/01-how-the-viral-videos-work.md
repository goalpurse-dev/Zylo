# How the viral "In this town" videos work

Study only. No story, names or assets from these videos go into our product.

**What was studied.**
- All 39 reference videos were measured in code: cuts, camera moves, word timings and pace.
- 14 videos were studied shot by shot, every contact sheet and transcript (videos 1-5, 10-13 and 35-39 in file order).
- About 10 more have partial notes, in `reference/blocky-worlds/analysis/_study-2026-11-24/`.
- I stopped the remaining shot-by-shot reading to save usage. The 14 agree so strongly that more reading would not change the plan.

## The short version

The references look rich but are built cheaply and safely. Each video uses a few sets, one or two people per shot, and almost no physical contact. Every spoken noun gets its own short shot. The camera always moves a little. Faces carry the emotion.

They are not perfect: each has 3 to 8 visible errors, such as bodies overlapping in a hug, a prop through an arm, or a light jump. But they never have the errors that make our videos unwatchable. Nobody is inside a wall. No main character goes missing. No named thing is left unshown more than once.

## The numbers

| What | Reference videos |
|---|---|
| Length | 66-195 s, median about 131 s (our test stories are 45 s) |
| Pace | Two styles: about 4.3 words/s with shots of about 1.3 s, or about 2.7 words/s with shots of about 1.8 s |
| Shots | 45-160 per video. 77% of cuts fall on the start of a spoken word. About one new shot per sentence, and one per item in a list |
| Camera move | Push-in about 50% of shots, tilt 19%, track 10%. Almost never static |
| Shot size | Close 19-36%, medium 37-63%, wide 12-34% |
| People in frame | Most shots show 0 or 1 person (74-98% in most videos). Two-person shots 5-30%. 3+ acting is rare |
| Places | 1-3 built sets carry 80-100% of the shots in most videos. Videos with 6-12 places give the extra places 1-4 shots each |
| Same framing repeated | The same setup comes back 4-35 times per video; only the face or the light changes |
| Spoken line shown literally | 83-93% literal, the rest a face reacting. 0-1 lines per video where the named thing is never shown |
| Rule object | On screen in the first 0-3 s, before or as it is named. Visible in 32-70% of all shots |
| Story | Rule stated by 3-10 s. 2-7 examples. Problem starts at 16-42% of runtime. Twist turns the rule itself. The last line calls back to line 1 |
| How people enter | The cut simply finds them already in place in 76-97% of shots. Walk-ins are short and toward or away from the camera |
| Contact between people | 0-6 shots per video, except one piggyback video |
| Creature with a person | 0-17 shots per video, never touching |

## The 18 things they all do

1. Show the rule as **one physical thing** (a line, a light, a ring, a glass body, a clock) in the first seconds, and keep it in a third to two thirds of shots.
2. Give **every spoken noun its own shot**. A list of three things gets three short shots.
3. Show a feeling, "I realised" or a sound as a **face close-up**. The face sticker does the acting.
4. Put **one person** in most shots, two in some, and a crowd almost never.
5. **Cut to people already standing in place.** Hardly anyone walks into frame.
6. Shoot **almost everything in 1-3 sets**, relit for day, dusk, night or danger.
7. **Reuse the same camera setups** many times.
8. **Keep the camera moving slightly**, mostly a slow push-in.
9. **Cut on the spoken word**, giving each sentence or noun a new shot of about 1-2 s.
10. **Never show contact as motion.** A hand-over is the holder, then a cut, then the receiver holding the thing. A hug is seen from behind. A hit is a held pose, a flash, then the result.
11. **Never animate a door, sitting down, standing up or picking up.** They cut to the result.
12. **Keep big creatures apart from people.** The creature is behind them with a clear gap, or in its own shot, or only shown as a silhouette, eyes, legs behind roofs, or part of the set.
13. Make **props large and attached** to one hand. Nothing small or fiddly.
14. **Put exposition into the picture as text**: signs, clocks, tally boards, phone screens, popups.
15. Show **time and danger through light**: a colour grade, night blue, red alarm. A flashback is the same set recoloured.
16. Make **"everyone" 3-14 people**, never a real crowd.
17. Use **a few recurring characters** (2-5) with **one fixed signature look** each, such as a scarf or a cap.
18. Use **the same story shape**: rule, examples, me, problem at about a third, discovery, a climax that uses the rule, and a last line that flips line 1.

## What they never try

- Two hands meeting, or a prop passing from hand to hand on screen.
- A creature touching a person.
- Two people walking and gesturing together in one moving shot.
- Travel shown as a journey. They hard-cut or wipe to the next place.
- A furnished room with several people acting in it.
- A real crowd doing what the narration says.
- Fingers, eating, writing, or a door opened on screen.
- An action held long enough to inspect it (contact beats last 0.8-2 s).
- A character breaking the story's own rule, unless that is the plot.

## What this means for us

Our engine tries to do what the references avoid: continuous scenes, characters walking between marks, hand-overs, a large monster standing among people, several people per shot, and any place or action the writer names. The references get their quality from a small stage, simple staging, strict word-to-picture cutting and faces. That is the target.
